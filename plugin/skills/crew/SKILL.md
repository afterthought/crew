# Managing crew's main levels

Teams and partitions are data in `~/.config/crew/teams.json` (version 2, 3 or 4), which the machine's configuration writes. Each role is an agent definition in `plugin/roles/`, naming its model and effort; its brief is built from that data. Nothing about a partition lives anywhere else. Each agent's brief is its system prompt, and the plan, the kits and the commits are the state, so restarting an agent is cheap and is the normal cure.

A bolt's work is started by `crew bolt start <bolt>`, the user's or a proposal's, never yours, and runs with the team that builds its kit in a stream; a bolt that is proven, every unit and chore merged and its verification green, waits to land, a row of its kit's landings card, with a fix when its landing conflicts with main, or when that card's forecast, made again whenever main moves, finds it in conflict with main: its row then takes main first, with no box, until a fix taking main in moves its head. A bolt is the branch `bolt/<bolt>` on GitHub, which `crew bolt start` has sluice make from the kit's main (`sluice branch create`) and `crew bolt land` and `crew bolt drop` have sluice delete (`sluice branch delete`), a dropped bolt's kept and named, the user's, while main lacks its tip and deleted once main holds it, with no worktree, and moves only through sluice: units, chores and fixes merge into it by `wt submit bolt/<bolt>`, and it lands by main-level ops's submission of it to main on the user's send on its kit's landings card (`crew bolt submit <bolt>...`, one kit's bolts in the card's order, each closed as it lands), which sluice pushes to GitHub; a landing pushes nothing and levels no host's checkout, since every kit's main checkout is an anchor the fleet's timer moves; it fetches the bolt and submits GitHub's head, first levelling the host's copy of `bolt/<bolt>` to it, guarded by the value it read, and saying so, submits a legacy host tip ahead of GitHub's copy as it stands, and refuses a bolt whose two copies have diverged, naming both commits, what each lacks and the way on: its team's fix, cut from GitHub's head, takes the host copy's merges in (`git merge bolt/<bolt>`) and merges into the bolt through sluice, which crew lets through since its head holds the host's tip, and the landing then submits GitHub's head; `crew bolt start` refuses, changing nothing, a kit whose `.config/sluice.toml` on `origin/main` names no checks or no `remote` for its bolt branches, and a host whose sluice is older than 0.20.0, which has no `branch create`. Every place is cut from `origin/bolt/<bolt>` after a fetch, and a bolt on GitHub is read there, its head, verification, changes, chores and stages, never at the host's own `bolt/<bolt>`, sluice's copy, which may lag; `crew bolts` and a start fetch it first, and every other read takes it as the host last fetched it; a bolt made before sluice made bolts' branches stays local until it lands, its places cut from the local branch. crew readies no worktree: a unit's, chore's and fix's places are Flume's, readied by its kind and the kit's own files. The bolt's units are built side by side as Flume places of the change kind, construct, code and verify, each submitted to the bolt through sluice, with no review between construct and code; crew starts no stage and waits on none, and a stage's start and end are Flume's, read from the runner's records. A bolt may also hold **chores**: planned maintenance that changes nothing a spec says must be true, built in its bolt as Flume places of the chore kind, with no construct and no review, and marked `chore` in `crew bolts`. A fix of a started bolt is a Flume place of the fix kind on it, `crew fix <bolt> <name> "<what is wrong>"`, dropped with `--drop`, the planner's or main-level ops's; none of it is yours. A bolt's proof is its verification: sluice's verdict on its last landing, which `crew bolts` shows, and its row is offered on its kit's landings card once it is green. A landing that conflicts with main lands nothing and moves nothing, and comes back to the bolt as a fix that merges main into its place and is submitted to the bolt as any fix is. In a kit whose main takes pull requests, a landing that does not land leaves the bolt's pull request into main a draft, which sluice never takes into main's queue, and a fix's start and every merge into the bolt keep it one, drafting it first; the next `crew bolt submit`, on the user's word, readies it.

Every team's work runs through **Flume**: version 3 of the teams file, and version 4 after it, names `flume` on every team, the Flume stream its work runs in on its host, or `false` where it names none, and a version-2 file names none; `crew bolts` says it on the bolt's head line, `in Flume stream <stream>`. crew runs no team's conductor, ops or slot: a team's work runs in Flume places on its host. A start is refused for a bolt started already, while its host is held, while no team builds its kit in a stream, while no runner answers there (no `<label>/runner/<host>` branch) or while the kit's policy names no checks; it marks the bolt `Path: flume` and appends to `requests.rec` on the flywheel's branch one launch for each unit and chore, which the host's runner turns into Flume places, a dependent launched once its `After` work has landed on the bolt, and starts no agent. Each host's runner answers on `<label>/runner/<host>` of the state repository, which crew fetches with the plan and never writes, and a piece's stage is read from it. Plan writes reach a bolt's places by their approval, an add, an amendment and a drop as launches, retries and drops; a move out, a split or an `After` change of launched work is refused, naming drop and add. `crew answer <work> "<words>"` answers a Flume place that stopped short or waits on its caller; `crew fix <bolt> <name> "<words>"` launches a fix place on a started bolt, and `--drop` drops it; a bolt's landing that sluice refuses for a conflict with main launches its take-main fix itself; and `crew main fix <kit> <name> "<the failure's words>"` launches a red main's fix as a Flume place on main, held for the user's send, where a team builds the kit in a stream. None of these is yours to run: they are the planner's, the design agent's and main-level ops's.

Each partition also has a main level: the design agent, the planner and ops in the `<label>` workspace of its session. The user reads the partition's constructed changes in batches across its bolts: when one is due, crew tells the planner, which starts the reviewer (`crew review <label>`, fresh in a `review` tab, ended once its summary is written); its summary has crew raise the batch's one card with its reading session in the planner's name, and the planner reads the user's notes with `crew card read batch/<n>` once the card's answer wakes it or crew tells it the reading was sent. The operator, `<label>-operator`, is the main level's fourth role and the partition's door; a main-level role runs in the session its partition's `roles` names for it, else the main level's. The console, the flow, rail and board tabs with no agent, is in each host's operator session, the session named `<label>`.

A main-level role may instead **run as its kind**, one role at a time, on the user's word: version 4 of the teams file names `flume` on every partition, an object naming each of its five roles, `design`, `planner`, `reviewer`, `ops` and `operator`, `false` while it stands or the Flume stream its runs run in on the main level's host, and in a file at version 2 or 3 every role stands. Such a role has no standing agent: the host's runner launches a run of it, a Flume place `crew-<role>--<label>` of the kind crew declares in `plugin/flume/kinds.toml`, whenever something is pending for it and none stands, one at a time. Whatever names it is an **ask**, a record in `asks.rec` on the flywheel's branch: a `crew tell` to it while no standing agent of it is up, crew's notices among them, a `crew send` to its operator, a capture, a bolt's landing or a landing that did not land, a change on a card in the run's name, which the host's follower hears from Pending You's notice (`pendingyou watch -- crew _answered`), `crew role open`, a batch `crew review` asks of the reviewer and one `crew review delivered` hands back to the planner; a place of crossed work that waits on the role is pending for it as an ask is. The run is the role, `<label>-<role>`, named from its place: `crew role start` takes everything pending as its turn and writes and prints its brief, the asks, the places that wait on it and the cards the user answered; `crew role end --report <path> "<what was done>"` ends the turn and writes its report; it waits on nothing in its session, and its work that lands is a place it launches with `crew role land <repo> <name> --brief-file <file>`, a Flume chore appended to `requests.rec`. Those three and `crew review delivered` are a run's own, refused anywhere else, and none is yours. For such a role `crew main up` opens no tab and starts nothing, writing its record in `roles.rec`, which the runner launches from, and ending a standing agent of it once it is not working; a standing agent of it is ended at its next stop and never restarted, `crew revive` and `crew update` pass it over, and `crew main status` says it runs as its kind, whether a run stands and how many asks wait. The user talks with such a role through `crew role open <role> --label <label>`.

A partition's plan, the signals its agents record, their moves and its run record are files on its flywheel's branch, `<label>/main`, of the state repository the teams file names for it (`state`). They are written only through crew. The planner changes the plan only by proposals the user approves (`crew plan proposed` lists them; `crew plan approve <n>` is run only on the user's word, with `--clear <unit>` for each new unit or chore the user leaves out), and you write none of it. A proposal's Pending You card is crew's: `crew plan propose` raises it in the planner's name, a rewrite changes it in place, and the approval or the drop closes it. It asks only the units and chores the proposal makes new, each a box ticked as proposed that the user clears to leave it out, what else it changes drawn on its face and applied with it, and a proposal with none is a hold-to-approve the user declines with a note to keep what it drops or amends; the planner records each answer the user gives on it with `crew plan answer <n> <question> <answer>`, and crew applies the change with the boxes as they stand once every question is answered. A unit already in a bolt can be amended: an approved proposal's `unit amend` (or the user's `crew unit amend`) changes its intent, and its place's construct is run again with it, with nothing to approve, its revised change in the next batch. A new partition's branch is made once with `crew state init <label>`, which adopts any `plan/<label>` its blueprints repos still have.

The command is `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/bin/crew`. A command about a main level runs on its host wherever you run it.

## Keeping the main level healthy

- **A dead agent** shows `not up` in `crew main status`. After a host restarts, `crew revive` brings back every main level's standing agent there whose pane came back empty, with its conversation, in the folder that conversation began in, or fresh and greeted when it has none, so it has one for the next revive, and leaves alone what was taken down. The command resets the pane first, because a Claude that dies badly leaves the terminal reporting mouse movement, which types escape codes into the shell. A pane whose folder was gone when herdr started comes back dead, listed with no terminal behind it: `crew main up` and `crew revive` close it and open it again in its workspace and say so, and `crew revive` then picks up its agent there.
- **A team** has no conductor, ops or slot to keep healthy: its work runs in Flume places on its host, and `crew bolts` shows each one's stage.
- **A role that runs as its kind** has no standing agent to keep healthy: `crew main status` and the board say how it stands, and the board's attention names a run stopped short, with its words, or lost, with its place. `crew-role` refuses it, naming `crew role open`; that is no fault. Starting a run again is the operator's or the user's, with `flume retry`.
- **Never act on an agent that is `working`** unless the user says so. `--force` exists for that.
- **A brief or the teams file changed.** Agents take their brief and crew's plugin at launch. crew restarts each standing agent a change made due at its next stop, once it is idle, with its conversation, and its band says when one is due; `crew update` hands the idle ones theirs now. Never restart one by hand that crew will restart.
- **Before herdr is upgraded or a host rebooted**, the user holds it: `crew hold <host> "<why>"`, then `crew hold wait <host>` once nothing more should start, then the upgrade, then `crew go <host>`. Hold, wait and go only on the user's word.
- **Blocked** means a question for the user. Read the pane and tell the user which agent is waiting and on what. Do not answer it for them. A tell to an agent waiting on a question is refused with nothing typed, so report the question first, and tell the agent again once the user has answered it.
- **A stuck stage.** A stage is Flume's: a place that stopped short asking, or waits on its caller, says so on its line in `crew bolts`, and the planner, the design agent or main-level ops answers it with `crew answer`; nothing of it is yours.

## What is not yours

You keep the main levels running. You do not do their work or steer it: no plan written, no design answers, no edits to a kit's repositories. If an agent has gone off course, tell the user what you saw. Text sitting in an agent's input box is usually Claude Code's suggested prompt, not something the user typed; never submit it. In a pane, a message beginning `[crew tell from <name>]` was sent with crew tell, one from `<user>@board` being the user's word from the board on the item it names, and one beginning `[crew]` by crew itself.

## Defining a team

A team or partition is declared in the machine's configuration, which writes `~/.config/crew/teams.json`; crew never edits that file. A team names its system, machine, herdr session, `units` and repos (its kits, one or more, then the blueprints repo, as names: a bolt is of one kit, and the team takes a bolt of any it holds); its partition is its session's. A partition names its label, its swancloud partition, its blueprints repos as `owner/name` (the first is its default), the machine and session of its main level, and its batch setting (`review`, `every` changes or `hours`). In version 3 every team also names `flume`, `false` or the name of its Flume stream, and in version 4 every partition names `flume` too, each of its five main-level roles `false` or the name of the Flume stream its runs run in. Either may override a role's model or effort under `roles`. Machines and sessions must already be declared as herdr hosts. Every field is read by `plugin/lib/crew.py`; `python3 plugin/lib/crew.py brief <team|label> <definition>` prints a brief, and missing or inconsistent data is an error, never a default.
