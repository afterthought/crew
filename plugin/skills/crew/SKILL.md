# Managing teams with crew

Teams and partitions are data in `~/.config/crew/teams.json` (version 2), which the machine's configuration writes. Each role is an agent definition in `plugin/roles/`, naming its model and effort; its brief is built from that data. Nothing about a team lives anywhere else. Each agent's brief is its system prompt, and the plan, the kits and the commits are the state, so restarting an agent is cheap and is the normal cure.

A team builds one bolt at a time, and may hold one more that waits to land: once its bolt is proven, every unit and chore merged and its verification green, `crew bolt give` gives it its next bolt beside it, and refuses while a bolt it holds is not proven or it holds two; the team builds the one given last, and the one waiting keeps only its reading, as a row of its kit's landings card, and a fix when its landing conflicts with main. Its standing roles are the **conductor** and **ops**, in the `<team>` workspace's **conductor** tab; on a Mac, its **git** tab holds gitgui in the kit and the blueprints repo, for the user, with no agent (a team on a remote host has no git tab: gitgui is unusable over ssh). A bolt is the branch `bolt/<bolt>` on GitHub, which `crew bolt give` has sluice make from the kit's main (`sluice branch create`) and `crew bolt land` and `crew bolt drop` have sluice delete (`sluice branch delete`), a dropped bolt's kept and named, the user's, while main lacks its tip and deleted once main holds it, with no worktree, and moves only through sluice: units, chores and fixes merge into it by `wt submit bolt/<bolt>`, and it lands by main-level ops's submission of it to main on the user's send on its kit's landings card (`crew bolt submit <bolt>...`, one kit's bolts in the card's order, each closed as it lands), which sluice pushes to GitHub; a landing pushes nothing and levels no host, since every kit's main checkout is an anchor the fleet's timer moves; `crew bolt give` refuses, changing nothing, a kit whose `.config/sluice.toml` on `origin/main` names no checks or no `remote` for its bolt branches, and a host whose sluice is older than 0.20.0, which has no `branch create`. Every place is cut from `origin/bolt/<bolt>` after a fetch; a bolt made before gives went through sluice stays local until it lands, its places cut from the local branch. The conductor and ops start in the kit's main checkout, or in the worktree a bolt given before bolts were branches still has, and `crew bolt give` to a team that is up restarts both, at once when it held no bolt and at their next stop beside a bolt that waits to land. Every worktree crew makes is made ready through its kit's `.config/sluice.toml`, its declared toolchain and then its runner's setup, before an agent starts there; a kit that declares nothing has nothing run. The bolt's units are built side by side, stage by stage (construct, code, verify, one round of code fixing what the verify's report marks fix, merge into the bolt with the change archived first, and a settle stage when a forecast conflict or a revised unit reaches one that waits to merge, a settle that takes another unit's work putting its unit after that one), with no review between construct and code, each stage a fresh agent in one of the team's unit slots, `<team>-unit-<n>`, in the `<team> units` workspace, which exists only while a unit or fix is in flight. A bolt may also hold **chores**: planned maintenance that changes nothing a spec says must be true, built in its bolt on `chore/<chore>` by code, verify and merge, with no construct and no review, and marked `chore` in `crew bolts`. The conductor runs the stages (`crew unit run`, `crew chore run`) and the fixes (`crew fix`, which names the fix's bolt with `--bolt <bolt>` while the team holds two); that is its work, not yours. A bolt's proof is its verification: sluice's verdict on its last landing, which `crew bolts` shows, and the conductor offers the landing once it is green. crew sends a stage's prompt and every greeting only once the agent is ready for input, reads its transcript to see it taken, and sends it once more when it was not; a stage whose prompt is not taken twice never started, with nothing left typed in its pane, and the conductor runs it again. A stage ends at what it delivers (construct's change, code's ticked tasks, verify's report, settle's commit, merge's commit on the bolt with a unit's change archived, a fix's commits), never when its agent goes quiet; a verify whose agent ends its turn with no report saved has not ended, and its wait says so then, never calling it stuck, and a unit merges only once its verify has delivered its report. A unit has merged only once its bolt holds its change archived: one carried there open inside another unit's merge keeps its place, `crew status` names it carried, and its own merge is owed; a merge whose branch holds another unit's change open that the bolt does not hold archived is refused, naming it. A landing that conflicts with main lands nothing and moves nothing, and comes back to the team as a fix that merges main into its place and is submitted to the bolt as any fix is. In a kit whose main takes pull requests, a landing that does not land leaves the bolt's pull request into main a draft, which sluice never takes into main's queue, and a fix's start and every merge into the bolt keep it one, drafting it first; the next `crew bolt submit`, on the user's word, readies it. The conductor waits for each through crew (`crew unit wait`, `crew chore wait`, `crew fix <team> <name> --wait`), which also says, before its answer, a conflict or failed check sluice records for the stage's submission, read from `sluice overview --json`, and a stage's agent that can't finish says what it needs with `crew needs "<words>"`, which ends the stage short and gives the conductor the words; a unit's merge that stops short for archives it can make itself, changes its branch holds open with every task ticked that no unit has and that its archive builds on, is refused once, naming them.

Each partition also has a main level: the design agent, the planner and ops in the `<label>` workspace of its session, and a dispatcher on each host its teams run on. The user reads the partition's constructed changes in batches across its teams: when one is due, crew tells the planner, which starts the reviewer (`crew review <label>`, fresh in a `review` tab, ended once its summary is written); its summary has crew raise the batch's one card with its reading session in the planner's name, and the planner reads the user's notes with `crew card wait batch/<n>`. The operator, `<label>-operator`, is the main level's fourth role and the partition's door; a main-level role runs in the session its partition's `roles` names for it, else the main level's. The console, the flow, rail and board tabs with no agent, is in each host's operator session, the session named `<label>`.

A partition's plan, the signals its agents record, their moves and its run record are files on its flywheel's branch, `<label>/main`, of the state repository the teams file names for it (`state`). They are written only through crew. The planner changes the plan only by proposals the user approves (`crew plan proposed` lists them; `crew plan approve <n>` is run only on the user's word, with `--clear <unit>` for each new unit or chore the user leaves out), and you write none of it. A proposal's Pending You card is crew's: `crew plan propose` raises it in the planner's name, a rewrite or a conductor's agreement changes it in place, and the approval or the drop closes it. It asks only the units and chores the proposal makes new, each a box ticked as proposed that the user clears to leave it out, what else it changes drawn on its face and applied with it, and a proposal with none is a hold-to-approve the user declines with a note to keep what it drops or amends; the planner records each answer the user gives on it with `crew plan answer <n> <question> <answer>`, and crew applies the change with the boxes as they stand once every question is answered. A unit already in a bolt can be amended: its conductor runs construct again with the user's words, or an approved proposal's `unit amend` (or the user's `crew unit amend`) changes its intent and starts its construct again at once in the team's slot, telling the conductor. Either way `crew bolts` shows it `amended`, then `construct`, and once that construct has committed it goes on from its tasks, with nothing to approve, its revised change in the next batch. A new partition's branch is made once with `crew state init <label>`, which adopts any `plan/<label>` its blueprints repos still have.

The command is `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/bin/crew`. A command about a team runs on the team's host wherever you run it.

## Keeping a team healthy

- **Context.** The conductor or ops above 40% context, or with any compaction, is due. The conductor restarts fresh with no loss. Ops holds findings and conversations that may not be committed yet: read the pane first, prefer `resume` after a crash, and `clear` only once what it knows is written down. **Never clear, restart or end a unit slot's agent** mid-stage: the conductor starts each stage fresh itself; if one's context runs high, tell the user.
- **A dead agent** shows `not up`. `crew resume` brings the conductor or ops back with its conversation, in the folder that conversation began in; if there is none it starts fresh and greets it, so it has one for the next resume. After a host restarts, `crew revive` does the same for every standing agent there whose pane came back empty, and leaves alone what was taken down. The command resets the pane first, because a Claude that dies badly leaves the terminal reporting mouse movement, which types escape codes into the shell. A pane whose folder was gone when herdr started comes back dead, listed with no terminal behind it: `crew up`, `resume`, `restart`, `crew main up` and `crew revive` close it and open it again in its workspace and say so, and `crew revive` then picks up its agent there. A slot whose agent died is the conductor's to run again.
- **`free`** means a slot holds no unit, chore or fix. That is normal; starting work in it is the conductor's call, not yours.
- **Never act on an agent that is `working`** unless the user says so. `--force` exists for that.
- **A brief or the teams file changed.** Agents take their brief and crew's plugin at launch. crew restarts each standing agent a change made due at its next stop, once it is idle, a conductor fresh and every other with its conversation, and its band says when one is due; `crew update` hands the idle ones theirs now. Never restart one by hand that crew will restart.
- **Before herdr is upgraded or a host rebooted**, the user holds it: `crew hold <host> "<why>"`, then `crew hold wait <host>` once nothing more should start, then the upgrade, then `crew go <host>`. Hold, wait and go only on the user's word.
- **Blocked** means a question for the user. Read the pane and tell the user which agent is waiting and on what. Do not answer it for them. A tell to an agent waiting on a question is refused with nothing typed, so report the question first, and tell the agent again once the user has answered it.
- **A stuck stage.** The conductor's wait reports a stage stuck when its agent has been quiet past crew's limit with nothing delivered, nothing said through `crew needs` and no background work of its own running. The conductor tells the user; the stage is run again, or waited on again, only on the user's word.

## What is not yours

You keep teams running. You do not do a team's work or steer it: no stages started, no plan written, no design answers, no edits to a team's repositories. If a conductor has gone off course, restart it, and if that does not fix it, tell the user what you saw. Text sitting in an agent's input box is usually Claude Code's suggested prompt, not something the user typed; never submit it. In a pane, a message beginning `[crew tell from <name>]` was sent with crew tell, and one beginning `[crew]` by crew itself.

## Defining a team

A team or partition is declared in the machine's configuration, which writes `~/.config/crew/teams.json`; crew never edits that file. A team names its system, machine, herdr session, number of unit slots (`units`) and repos (its kits, one or more, then the blueprints repo, as names: a bolt is of one kit, and the team takes a bolt of any it holds); its partition is its session's. A partition names its label, its swancloud partition, its blueprints repos as `owner/name` (the first is its default), the machine and session of its main level, and its batch setting (`review`, `every` changes or `hours`). Either may override a role's model or effort under `roles`. Machines and sessions must already be declared as herdr hosts. Every field is read by `plugin/lib/crew.py`; `python3 plugin/lib/crew.py brief <team|label> <definition>` prints a brief, and missing or inconsistent data is an error, never a default.
