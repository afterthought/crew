## Context and problem statement

An agent waits for three kinds of event: a sluice submission's outcome, a unit's or chore's stage end, and a card's answer. Today each wait is a process polling on the agent's behalf: `crew unit wait` reads the stage every ten seconds, `crew card wait` asks Pending You every five, and `sluice wait` polls sluice's files and drives its rounds besides. Each belongs to the session that started it and to nobody once that session ends: on 2026-10-28 nine batch waits ran orphaned for 33 hours on deneb, and two merges of crw-6's bolt sat passed in sluice with no waiter to land them. After a Claude Code session restarts, by crew, by herdr or by the user, nothing reconnects: the session comes back cold and learns of its submissions, stages and cards only when told. Beside the waits, per-session watchers (fifty sluice sidebar watchers on deneb) and crew's own followers polled the host into that day's meltdown.

The user asks for one design: events pushed into the session, not polled; a restarted session reconnecting to its waits from the records; and each piece living where it belongs.

## Considered options

1. Every wait is one background command of the session's own that blocks until the event and returns, the harness bringing the session back; every wait recorded where the records hold its subject; a session's start reconnecting from those records by crew's SessionStart hook (the mod) and the brief's instruction; sluice's and Flume's pushes theirs, crew the wrapper (chosen).
2. A crew daemon that watches everything and prompts the sessions. Rejected: `state.4` (crew runs no service), the user's word against kludge in crew, and it would be the one process whose crash loses every wait.
3. The waits as they are, polling, given timeouts. Rejected: the polls are the cost, and nothing reconnects.
4. Flywheel's event bus. Rejected by the user: Flywheel is not the plan.

## Decision outcome

Option 1, in seven parts.

1. **An agent submits, launches or posts, and goes on; the event reaches it as the tool's push into its pane** (record 0048, pattern 5). A submission's outcome, a stage's end and a card's answer are each delivered once, by the tool that decides them, as a nudge through herdr naming the record (pattern 3); the agent reads the record and acts on what it finds. A command that blocks on the event in the session's background is the session's own choice only where nothing on its host pushes that fact, a host that runs no queue or a stage's end before Flume's settle runs there, and is never made beside a push for the same fact on the same host: one fact, one delivery. crew keeps no waiting process of its own: no `crew card wait`, and no wait that outlives the session that started it; a wait whose session ends ends with it. Everything of this part that is crew's is the wrapper's: what it asks for at a start, what it reads, what it stops doing.

2. **Every wait is recorded where its subject lives.** A merge's or a landing's submission number is on the bolt's row from the moment of the submit; a stage's wait is on the row with what it waits for and since when (`teams.22`); a card is Pending You's record under its key. Nothing a session waits on is kept only in that session's memory. crew's.

3. **A start asks for what is untold to it, and that is a launched role's own start, not a crew hook** (record 0048, pattern 4; record 0047, move 6; the user's note on this record's card, 2026-10-28). Nothing is built in crew for it: once Flume launches a role on a nudge, that run's start asks each tool for its untold outcomes, sluice's one command, Flume's ask (flume 0009) and `list_pending`, before it acts; until then a crew agent that comes back reads its bolt's rows and its open cards as it does today, and starts again only a wait nothing on its host pushes, a stage wait of today's (part 6). No SessionStart hook, no greeting list and no reconnect of crew's own, since the roles that would carry them leave with the teams.

4. **A card's answer is pushed by Pending You's wake mod** (record 0048, pattern 3), as today: the answer wakes the owner's session, which acts on it; after a restart `list_pending` at the start reconnects (pattern 4), and the hook's list carries the cards too. crew reads an answer at the owner's act and at the rail's refresh, never by a wait of its own. swancloud's mod, built; crew's reading.

5. **A submission's outcome is sluice's push; a host's queues move where the fleet runs sluice for them** (sluice record 0068 as revised on the user's note: sluice stays a plain CLI, and nobody is told to set up a daemon). Nothing of sluice's need run. A host may run `sluice run`, by a fleet's service among other ways; where one runs, a wait or a watch that finds it live follows its document and runs no round, and where none runs a wait drives rounds as today, so a lone laptop is unchanged. On the fleet's hosts that choice is swancloud's own and its binding swancloud's (a service on deneb, the gate host and the boxes, the gate host's `sluice run` of record 0039 being the same), never sluice's ask. The round that decides an outcome pushes it into the submitter's session through herdr and records on the submission that it was told, when and to which session; an outcome not yet told stands in sluice's records as decided and untold, and a session that starts is told every untold outcome of the submissions it made, before it asks for anything. So no wait stands for an outcome to arrive: a merge or a landing crew submitted reaches the conductor's or ops's session as sluice's push, the one delivery of that outcome on a host where the fleet runs the queue, and crew acts on the told outcome as it acts on any tell, reading the record for the fact (`plan.21`). No agent on such a host runs `sluice wait` in its background beside the push (record 0048, pattern 5); whichever of a push and a reporting wait comes first marks the outcome told, and the other delivers nothing again (sluice clause 51). `sluice wait <number>` in the session's background is for a host where nothing runs the queue, where the wait drives the rounds as today. sluice's push and records; the fleet's choice to run it; crew's act on a told outcome.

6. **A stage's end is Flume's push** (flume record 0009): the settle that reads a stage's end tells each waiter on the place once, through the terminal into the waiter's own pane, and records on the place that it was told, to whom and when; a wait is by place, naming its stage or its submission, answers at once with an end untold to this waiter and otherwise follows the host's state under a timeout, driving no settle where the terminal keeps watch; a restarted caller runs the same wait and loses nothing, and a caller that starts may ask Flume for every end untold to it, which crew's hook does beside sluice's untold outcomes (part 3). Once Flume's settle pushes on a host, that push is the one delivery of a stage's end there, and no background wait is made beside it (record 0048, pattern 5; Flume clause 43: whichever of the push and a reporting wait comes first marks it told). Until crew launches through Flume (record 0047, move 3), nothing pushes a stage's end, so `crew unit wait` and `crew chore wait` stay as they are, run as a background command of the conductor's session, ending with that session and started again from the row at its start, which pattern 5 allows; no rewrite of the wait is made in crew, since move 3 deletes it whole. Flume's; crew's reading until then.

7. **Nothing watches per session.** A reading pulls the records on the host's clock (record 0048, pattern 1): a host runs one follower for the rail and the board (`plan.12`'s reading of 2026-10-28) and one sluice sidebar watcher, with backoff when its stream breaks (madswan's side); a Claude session keeps no watcher of its own. Tailrace carries nothing of this today: a deploy's wait is the deployer's, and tailrace's in the same shape when deploys move there.
