# Design

## Context

See proposal.md, *Why*. crew already starts a reading session for two of its cards: a batch's and a long proposal's. `card_built` asks the act for the session before it builds the card (`reading_of`, `READING`, `CardActs.session` → `reading_open` → `session_open`), and a session that can't open is said on the card (`unopened`). `row_card_close` ends the session of a `READING` kind when it closes the card, and the rail's sweep ends any session whose card is closed or whose row is gone, on every host of the partition (`sweep_sessions`). `session_open` gives a running session's link again while plannotator lists it as serving the same files, and otherwise starts plannotator on the files a writer hands it, with `PLANNOTATOR_REMOTE=1` and no browser, refusing a link only the host could open; the link reaches the tailnet through the host's `PLANNOTATOR_URL_HOST` and `PLANNOTATOR_PORT`, which swancloud sets (on mac-studio, `auto` and `19432-19451`).

A landing has none of this. `session_refusal` refuses `land/` keys, "a landing card has nothing to read"; `reading_of` returns None for them; the rail's land row has `doc=None`; `cards.landing_card` takes no link. `raise_land` builds the card's facts on the team's host, in the conductor's name, from the plan, the kit and the run record, and is called at a merge's end and by the rail's sweep for a row whose card is missing or lost.

Rules this rests on: `briefs.9` (a landing's one reading session on the bolt's changes against main, offered beside the approval and gating nothing, its link in the card's words first and among its links, never a browser on the host, its annotations read as the card's notes, ended when the card closes), `briefs.13` (the landing's form, an approval of risk high, linking that session, offered and never required; one card per thing, changed in place), `briefs.11` and `briefs.16` (crew raises and changes the card itself, in the conductor's name), `plan.16` (the approval of the landing card, passed on by the conductor, is the word to land), `plan.18` (main is read as `origin/main` after a fetch), `read.1` (what can't be read is refused and named), `read.4` (crew ends only a process it started), `code.7` (the decisions are functions of facts), `state.4`. Record 0031 §3, amended and accepted 2026-10-19, and §5's landing entry. Pages whose areas this touches: `plan.md` (`plan.py`), `briefs.md` (`conductor.md`, the crew skill), `teams.md` (`plugin/bin/crew`'s usage), `tests.md` and `docs.md` (README, specs).

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** one session per landing card, on what the bolt brings to main, started with the card on the team's host, followed when the head moves, restarted by `crew card session`, its notes reaching the conductor, ended with the card.

**Non-Goals:**
- The bolt's preview addresses from sluice (record 0031 §3's amendment of 2026-10-20): a unit of their own.
- Any change to the card's approval, its face's counts and list, or who lands (`plan.16`).
- Changing a batch's or a proposal's card when their session restarts at a new link: they keep today's behaviour (named in *Risks*).

## Decisions

**1. The session runs on the team's host, beside the card.** The landing card is raised and closed on the team's host, where the conductor's cards are and the kit's main checkout is (`raise_land`, `landing_cards_closed`), so its session runs there too: written from that checkout's git, ended by the same `row_card_close`, swept by the same host's `crew card sweep`. `crew card session land/<bolt>` run on another host is refused naming the team's host, as a batch's is refused off the main level's host. *Considered:* the main level's host, as a batch's: it would read the kit over ssh and its session would outlive the card's close on the team's host until a sweep. *Considered:* forwarding the command to the team's host (`forward_card`): it would hold a landing to a rule its batch sibling does not keep; the refusal names where to run it.

**2. One review: a cover page, every unit's change, then the diff.** The session is one `plannotator annotate` of several files, as a batch's is, so it opens as one review with one decision, and `session_open` stays as it is:
- `00-<bolt>.md`, written by crew: the bolt, its kit, its head, the units and chores that land, one a line, and two sentences: that this reading lands nothing and the card's approval is the word to land; that what the user sends from it reaches the conductor as notes on the card, and a note naming a defect holds the landing for a fix.
- `<kk>-<unit>-<path>.md` for the k-th unit in the plan's order, each Markdown file of its change as the head holds it, archived (its merge archives it on the bolt) or open, in a change's reading order (`change_order`), named as a batch names them.
- `<nn>-<bolt>-diff.txt`, the diff `git diff <base> <head>`, where `<base>` is the merge-base of `origin/main` after a fetch and the head (`plan.18`, as `raise_land` reads `moved`), leaving out `openspec/changes/`, whose Markdown the unit files already show whole; specs, code and every chore's commits stay in it. A chore has no change of its own, so it is read in the diff and named on the cover.

plannotator 0.28.23, on mac-studio on 2026-10-19, refused `annotate a.md b.diff` ("File type not supported: .diff … Every file in a review of several files must be one annotate opens") and opened `annotate a.md c.txt` as one review of both: the diff goes in as `.txt`. *Considered:* `plannotator review --patch-file`, plannotator's diff view: it takes no other file in the same session, has no `--gate` or `--result-file`, and so would be a second session and a second wait, where `briefs.9` and the user ask for one. *Considered:* the diff inside a Markdown fence: not read against plannotator, and a fence of thousands of lines is what the batch's summary was taken out of a code block for (`briefs.13`, the phone's sideways scroll).

The files are written into `~/.local/state/crew/cards/<label>/land/<bolt>/<head7>/`, the head in the path, so a session on another head serves another document and `session_open` starts anew rather than giving the old link. Every file is read before any is written; what can't be read refuses, naming it, and nothing is written (`read.1`). Which files, in which order and with which names is a function of facts, `landing_reading(bolt, kit, head, units, chores, changes, diff)` → `[(name, text)]` (`code.7`), its reads thin around it.

**3. The card carries the link as a batch's does, and says it gates nothing.** `cards.landing_card` takes `link` (or None): its details open with "Read the bolt in one session: <link>" and a sentence that the reading lands nothing and the card's approval is the word to land, before the batches; its links and its artifacts carry `Read bolt <bolt>` first, then sluice's pull request when it has one. The title, the summary, the list of what lands, the approval and its rationale are as settled on 2026-10-15. `READING` gains `"land": "link"` and `reading_of` returns `"land"` for a `land/` key, so `card_built` starts the session, `unopened` says when it could not, and `row_card_close` ends it.

**4. The head moving restarts the session; a restart by hand changes the card.** `raise_land` already changes the card in place when a merge moves the head. With the head in the folder's path, `reading_open` for a `land/` key ends a recorded session whose document is not the new head's (as it does for a proposal's older page) and starts one on the new head, and the card's change carries the new link. `crew card session land/<bolt>` gives the running session's link again and changes nothing; when it starts the session anew (none recorded, or the recorded one no longer serves), it changes the open landing card in place with the note "Its reading is open again at a new link" while the card waits on the user, through `row_card_change` in the conductor's name, and when the user has already answered the card it leaves the card as it is and prints the link alone, since following an answered landing card with a new one would ask for the landing again. This is what gives a card raised before this change its link, the-box-works-in-wldn-prod's among them, and what keeps a link from going dead after the host restarts: plannotator takes a port from `PLANNOTATOR_PORT`'s range at each start (mac-studio's sessions on 2026-10-19 held 19432 to 19439), so a session started anew is usually at another address. *Considered:* print the new link alone, as a batch's restart does: the user's card would keep a link that no longer opens, against "it stays running, it closes when it's supposed to once we're done, it's accessible" (record 0031 §5, the batch's session).

**5. What the user sends from the session is notes, never the word to land.** `briefs.9` reads a session's annotations as its card's notes; the landing's notes are the conductor's to act on (a defect is a fix, `conductor.md`). The conductor runs `crew card wait land/<bolt>` in the background once it has adopted the card, and again each time the wait returns while the card is open. For a `land/` key the wait prints the session's decision with words that say what it is: annotated, "<key>: notes from its reading, as notes on the landing card:" then the user's words as plannotator gives them; approved, "<key>: approved in its reading, which lands nothing: the card's approval is the word to land", with any words after it; closed with no decision, as today. Having printed a decision, the wait sets the result aside (`result.<started>.json`, as `session_open` does for a dismissed one) and forgets the session record, the plannotator process having ended with the decision, so the next `crew card session` starts a new one and changes the card's link (Decision 4) and the next wait waits on it. The card is never closed by the wait. plannotator's `--gate` stays: `--result-file`, which the wait reads, requires it (`plannotator annotate --help`, 0.28.23), so the cover page says what its Approve does.

**6. Ended when the card closes.** `row_card_close` ends a `READING` kind's session; with `land` among them, `crew bolt land`'s close (`landing_cards_closed`), a fix's withdrawal (`landing_held`) and the sweep's close of a card whose row is gone each end it on the team's host, and the rail's `crew card sweep` ends one whose card closed elsewhere. The intent's "ends the session when the card is answered" is read as `briefs.9` has it, when the card closes: an approval is followed by the landing, which closes it; a note naming a defect by the fix, which withdraws it; and the session stays readable between the answer and that close.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [A bolt's diff is large: a lockfile or generated code can run to tens of thousands of lines] → plannotator opens it as one text file; nothing is cut, since a reading that leaves out part of what lands could mislead. If a real bolt shows it unreadable, a later change can leave out files a kit names.
- [The team's host sets no `PLANNOTATOR_URL_HOST`, as the box may not] → `session_open` refuses the loopback link, and the card is raised saying its reading could not be opened on the tailnet (`unopened`); the landing is not held.
- [The conductor forgets to wait on the session] → the annotations wait in the result file until the next wait, which returns them at once; the card itself is unaffected. A wait run once the card closed finds no session and says so.
- [A batch's and a long proposal's card still keep a dead link after their session restarts] → unchanged by this change; named for the planner.

## After deploy

- The first real landing card after this lands, on mac-studio, opened on the user's phone over the tailnet: the session shows the cover page, each unit's change and the diff, and opens; its doer the user, on their next landing card, since no check in crew's tiers reaches the phone or the tailnet. For the-box-works-in-wldn-prod, waiting now, swc-2-conductor runs `crew card session land/the-box-works-in-wldn-prod` once crew's main holds this, and the open card is changed to carry the link.
